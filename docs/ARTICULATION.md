# Training clearer explanations

[Documentation](README.md) · [Speak](SPEAK.md)

Recall uses **articulation** to mean expressing an idea so the intended listener can understand it.
This is educational explanation practice, not pronunciation assessment or speech therapy. Speak's
`articulation-1` evaluation criteria were reviewed on October 4, 2026.

> Explain → get specific feedback → practise one change → try again.
>
> This is a research-informed practice loop. It is not proof that AI judgments are reliable or that
> a positive evaluation establishes mastery.

## What the research supports

### Main point, structure and audience

The faculty-developed AAC&U Oral Communication VALUE rubric emphasizes a central message,
organization, audience-appropriate language and supporting material. Recall adapts those elements to
explanations of knowledge, rather than copying a presentation score. Its posture, gesture and
vocal-expression criteria cannot be assessed from a transcript and are excluded. This is an
educational framework, not proof that Recall's AI judgments are reliable. [Published
rubric](https://und.edu/academics/essential-studies/oralcommunicationrubric.html)

### Practice with specific feedback

A randomized, 56-participant, ten-day study of ROC Speak reported improvement in judged speaking
attributes with combined automated and peer feedback. The intervention bundled several kinds of
feedback; it does not isolate which element helped or validate an AI-only coach. It supports testing
a practice → specific feedback → retry loop. [Zhao et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08735)

### Fillers need context

Fillers are not inherently failures. In two English/Dutch experiments, hearing “uh” helped
recognition of upcoming words, while “um” was neutral. These narrow listening results do not mean
every filler helps; they do undermine an automatic zero-fillers rule. Recall keeps observed filler
counts separate from explanation judgments. [Fox Tree, 2001, author's
manuscript](https://people.ucsc.edu/~foxtree/Publications_files/foxtree.2001.ms.pdf)

### Choose a structure for the task

Stanford's communication teaching recommends audience-centered preparation and organizing
structures. These are practical teaching frameworks rather than validated timing prescriptions. Use
a structure that fits the task; a decision pitch and a technical mechanism need not follow the same
template. [Audience-centered
preparation](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/quick-thinks-all-effective-communication-must-start),
[organizing
structures](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/one-communication-tool-you-should-add-your-toolkit)

## How Speak applies it

| Existing dimension  | What the coach considers                                                                                                                                 |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Accuracy            | Agreement with the selected reference; errors, omissions and unsupported claims kept distinct. Fluency cannot establish correctness.                     |
| Depth               | Relevant mechanisms, relationships, examples and important conditions. Longer answers and more jargon do not automatically show understanding.           |
| Clarity & structure | An identifiable main point, useful sequence, explicit connections, precise wording and removal of distracting repetition. Brevity must preserve meaning. |
| Audience fit        | Appropriate assumed knowledge, term definitions, detail and concrete examples for this listener. Technical language can help a technical peer.           |

Each new evaluation adds **one focused practice rep**: a priority, why it matters to the listener,
an exact excerpt from the edited answer (or an explicit omission), a suggested revision, and one
drill with one to three observable self-checks. A strong explanation gets a stretch exercise, not an
invented problem. A supported factual error can take priority over style.

### One change per retry

The available drills are main-point first, explain with an example, try a different listener,
compress while preserving meaning, and repair one gap. These are product exercises, not
experimentally validated protocols. They have no mandatory word count, universal speaking speed or
prescribed pause length. Audience-switch retries actually select the new listener. Other retries
preserve the current setup.

On **Practice again**, a collapsed reminder carries the objective into the new attempt. Evaluation
receives the selected retry objective, not previous explanations or unrelated history. It judges the
new answer; it cannot claim improvement by comparing against an answer it has not received. The
reminder is transient; restarting or restoring a draft begins ordinary practice.

## Evidence and limits

Coaching quotes must match the edited transcript exactly. Recorded delivery observations continue to
use the original transcript and approximate audio measurements; editing prose cannot rewrite them.
No pronunciation, accent, confidence, anxiety or intelligence is inferred from text. Aggregate
timing cannot locate a pause within a quoted sentence.

Without a reference, accuracy remains **Not assessed**. Suggested wording can reorganize the
speaker's claims but cannot introduce a fact-checked answer. Pasted notes are an unverified
reference. A schema and source checks constrain output; they cannot guarantee a correct judgment.
OpenAI's evaluation guidance also warns about verbosity bias and recommends specific criteria and
representative evaluation cases. [Evaluation best
practices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices)

Older saved evaluations retain their original feedback. New attempts record the rubric version.
Practice does not infer mastery, rate cards or change schedules. Local automated and mocked desktop
checks establish contract, persistence and UI behavior; representative real explanations and
human-reviewed provider results are still needed to calibrate coaching quality.
